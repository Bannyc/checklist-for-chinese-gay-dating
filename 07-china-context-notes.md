# 07 - Mainland China Context Notes

Background notes to help you interpret the other files. These notes are general and may be out of date or simplified. **Verify current laws and services with official or professional sources.**

---

## 1. Legal landscape (high level)

- Same-sex relationships between consenting adults are not criminalized in mainland China (the old "hooliganism" offense that was sometimes applied was removed in the late 1990s).
- Same-sex marriage and civil unions are **not** legally recognized. This affects inheritance, property, medical decision-making, immigration, and parenting rights.
- Some couples use legal tools to gain partial protection, such as **wills**, **adult guardianship agreements (意定监护)**, and clear contracts about property. Consult a qualified lawyer for your situation.
- Legal routes for same-sex couples to have children are limited, and surrogacy is restricted in mainland China. Be cautious about overseas arrangements; they involve legal, ethical, and practical complexities.
- Online content rules can affect LGBT-related accounts and organizations. Be aware that community groups and social media accounts may be limited or removed at times.

## 2. Social context

- Attitudes vary widely by city, age, education, family, and workplace. Large cities often offer more community and anonymity; smaller cities can offer less.
- Many gay men are not out to family or colleagues. Treat **privacy as a shared, serious responsibility.**
- There is strong social expectation to marry and have children, often expressed through family pressure around festivals, matchmaking, and ages ("leftover" stigma).
- Conversion-type practices have been reported in some places; some people have experienced pressure or harmful "treatment" attempts. Be gentle when this comes up.
- Community and peer networks (friends, online groups, local organizations) are important sources of support and information.

## 3. Dating app and community culture

- Apps are a main way to meet people, with different cultures around hookups, dating, or friendship. Clarify what each person wants.
- Real-name registration rules and data handling vary by platform; assume that digital traces exist.
- Be aware of scams (see file 06) and of data privacy risks.
- Community terms differ from English-language gay culture; don't assume that role labels or terms mean exactly what they would elsewhere. Ask people what they mean.

## 4. Health system and services

- Local **CDC** offices, hospitals, and community organizations are common sources of HIV/STI testing and counseling. Policies on cost, anonymity, and availability vary by location.
- PrEP and PEP availability, access, and cost may vary by city and over time. Ask local community organizations or an infectious-disease doctor for the latest information.
- Hepatitis B is more common in China than in many countries, and many people are vaccinated or carry the virus. Learn your own status and talk openly, without stigma.
- Mental health services are expanding but can be uneven. University counseling centers, hospital psychiatry departments, and private counselors are options.

## 5. Family and festivals

- **Spring Festival** is often a stress point: pressure to return home, attend matchmaking, or hide relationships. Plan ahead, together, with kindness.
- Many people are expected to support or care for parents. Discuss expectations openly.
- "Filial piety" can be a deeply held value, not just pressure. Respect that while still seeking an honest plan.

## 6. Cooperative marriage (形婚) and related topics

- Some gay men and lesbian women consider arrangements to satisfy family expectations. These arrangements raise questions about honesty, legal obligations, health, and children.
- **Ethical principle:** Everyone involved should know the truth and consent. Deceiving a woman into marriage with a man who is gay is harmful.
- If your partner is considering this, discuss what it means for the relationship, for exclusivity, and for your own wellbeing. You are allowed to say it is not something you can accept.

## 7. Work and education

- Some employers and sectors (e.g., civil service, certain state-owned enterprises, education) may feel less safe for being out. Respect different risk levels.
- Long working hours are common in some industries; plan realistically for time together.

## 8. Practical safety and privacy

- Keep in mind that mutual friends, group chats, and social media can spread information quickly.
- Consider the privacy of photos, location sharing, and shared devices.
- Know the emergency numbers (police 110, ambulance 120) and trusted contacts.

## 9. Vocabulary (neutral, informal)

| Term | Rough meaning |
|------|---------------|
| 出柜 (chūguì) | Coming out |
| 形婚 (xínghūn) | "Cooperative marriage" between a gay and a lesbian person or other arrangement for appearances |
| 同妻 (tóngqī) | Wife of a gay man, often unaware when marrying |
| 意定监护 (yìdìng jiānhù) | Agreed (adult) guardianship arrangement |
| 疾控 (jíkòng) | Disease control and prevention center (CDC) |
| 暴露 (bàolù) | Exposure, including being outed |
