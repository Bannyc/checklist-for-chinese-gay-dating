# 02 - Personality, Temperament, and Communication

Compatibility is less about being similar and more about handling differences well.

*Suggested timing: developing stage. Observe stress, conflict, and routine, not only fun dates.*

---

## A. Emotional maturity

**Observe**
- [ ] They can name their feelings, not only act them out.
- [ ] They can calm down after anger, and come back to discuss.
- [ ] They take feedback without collapsing or attacking.
- [ ] They don't need constant reassurance, and don't withhold affection to punish.
- [ ] They can sit with uncertainty without panicking or controlling.

**Ask**
- "How do you usually deal with stress or bad days?"
- "What do you need from a partner when you're upset?"
- "What do you do when you're angry?"

**Concerning signs**
- Silent treatment as a regular tool
- Rage, throwing or breaking things, intimidation
- Extreme mood swings that you are always expected to manage
- Blaming you for their emotions ("you made me do this")

## B. Conflict style

- [ ] Can you disagree without it turning into insults or contempt?
- [ ] Do they listen to your side before responding?
- [ ] Can they apologize and repair?
- [ ] Do they bring up old mistakes as weapons?
- [ ] Do they avoid all conflict (which may mean resentment builds up)?

**Try this:** Notice how they handle a small inconvenience (a cancelled reservation, a delay). It shows more than a big romantic evening.

## C. Communication habits

| Question | Notes |
|---------|-------|
| Do they reply in reasonable time, or disappear for days and return with excuses? | |
| Do they communicate clearly what they want and don't want? | |
| Do they prefer messaging, calls, or in person? Is that compatible with you? | |
| Can you talk about difficult topics without them shutting down? | |
| Do they listen, or wait for their turn to speak? | |
| Do they ask about your life with real curiosity? | |

## D. Introversion, extroversion, and social energy

- [ ] How much social time and alone time does each of you need?
- [ ] Are your preferences for nightlife, travel, home life, or hobbies compatible?
- [ ] How do they feel about your friends, and you about theirs?

## E. Attachment and intimacy style

Without diagnosing anyone, observe tendencies:

- **Anxious pattern:** needs frequent contact, fears abandonment, interprets silence as rejection.
- **Avoidant pattern:** values independence strongly, pulls away when things get close.
- **Secure pattern:** comfortable with closeness and independence, communicates needs.

**Ask**
- "How much closeness and space do you like in a relationship?"
- "What makes you feel loved?" (words, time, acts, touch, gifts)

## F. Self-esteem and internalized stigma

Growing up gay in a heteronormative environment can leave marks. These are common and not a flaw, but worth understanding:

- [ ] Do they speak of themselves with constant shame or self-hatred?
- [ ] Do they put down other gay people to feel more "normal"?
- [ ] Are they able to enjoy the relationship, or do they treat it as shameful?
- [ ] Are they open to counseling or growth, if needed?

## G. Mental health and coping

- [ ] How do they handle depression, anxiety, or loneliness?
- [ ] Do they have healthy coping habits (friends, exercise, hobbies, therapy) or harmful ones (heavy drinking, drugs, risky behavior)?
- [ ] Are they willing to talk about mental health without shame?
- [ ] If either of you needs professional support, would you both be open to it?

> Needing support is not a defect. What matters is whether the person takes responsibility for their well-being and treats you well.

## H. Humor, curiosity, and shared joy

- [ ] Do you laugh together?
- [ ] Are you curious about each other's worlds?
- [ ] Do you enjoy ordinary moments (cooking, walking, doing nothing)?
- [ ] Do they support your growth rather than feel threatened by it?

## I. Quick personality compatibility prompts

Discuss together:

1. "What is your ideal weekend?"
2. "What is your biggest pet peeve in a partner?"
3. "How do you like to make decisions: together, or one person leads?"
4. "What did your parents' relationship teach you about love?"
5. "What would make you feel the relationship is failing?"

## J. Trust-building signals

- [ ] Their actions match their words over months.
- [ ] They introduce you to friends (at a pace you both agree on).
- [ ] They are consistent, not hot-and-cold.
- [ ] You feel more like yourself with them, not less.
