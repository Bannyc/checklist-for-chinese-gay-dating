# Know Before You Commit: A Getting-to-Know-You Guide for Young Gay Men in Mainland China

An open, community-editable guide to the things worth learning about a potential partner **before** you decide to be in an exclusive relationship: values, character, personality, health, family situation, finances, and safety.

> This is a **conversation guide, not an interrogation script or a purity test.** Use it to understand someone and to be understood in return. Every question here applies to *you* as much as to the other person.

---

## Who is this for?

Young gay men (roughly 18-35) in mainland China who are dating, or about to start dating, and want a more thoughtful way to decide whether someone is a good match. It may also be useful to bisexual and queer men, and the ideas adapt to other people with small edits.

## Why this exists

Dating as a gay man in mainland China comes with extra layers that most generic dating advice ignores:

- Many of us are not fully out, so **privacy and safety** matter a lot.
- There is **family pressure** to marry a woman and have children.
- Same-sex marriage is **not legally recognized**, so partners have fewer default legal protections.
- Much dating starts on **apps**, where profiles can be incomplete or fake, and scams exist.
- **Sexual health** conversations are often avoided, even though they are important.
- Social support and role models for healthy gay relationships can be limited.

This guide tries to fill that gap with practical, respectful, and non-judgmental checklists.

## Core principles

1. **Mutual, not one-sided.** Don't ask what you wouldn't answer yourself.
2. **Pace matters.** Not everything should be asked on a first chat. Each file marks suggested timing: *early*, *developing*, or *before committing*.
3. **Disclosure is a process.** Sensitive topics (health, family, past relationships) deserve trust and privacy. Never pressure someone to reveal what they are not ready to share, and never share what they told you without consent.
4. **Values over checkboxes.** A checklist cannot measure chemistry or trust. It helps you notice patterns and ask better questions.
5. **No stigma.** Living with a health condition (including HIV) does not make someone a worse partner. What matters is honesty, informed choices, and care for each other. See the health file.
6. **Your safety comes first.** If something feels wrong, you are allowed to leave, block, and not explain.
7. **Don't try to "fix" people.** Red flags are information, not challenges.
8. **Privacy is protection.** In a society where outing can cost people jobs and family relationships, treat other people's information as something you are guarding.

## What this guide is NOT

- It is **not medical, legal, or psychological advice.** For health questions see a doctor or a qualified community organization. For legal questions consult a lawyer.
- It is **not a ranking tool** for people, and it should not be used to shame, expose, or blacklist anyone.
- It is **not a guarantee.** Even careful people get hurt. The goal is better-informed decisions, not zero risk.

## Quick-start: the 10 most important things

If you read nothing else, make sure you have honest answers about:

1. Are they honest, including when it's inconvenient? (Check small things over time.)
2. Do they treat service staff, family, and exes with basic respect?
3. How do they handle disagreement and stress?
4. Are they single, and truthfully so? Is there any plan to marry a woman?
5. Do you both agree on exclusivity and what it means?
6. Are you both willing to talk about sexual health and get tested regularly?
7. How out are they, and is that compatible with what you need?
8. Do your life directions (city, career, children, family duties) fit?
9. Do you feel safe and relaxed with them, not anxious and walking on eggshells?
10. Do they respect your "no", about sex, privacy, money, and everything else?
